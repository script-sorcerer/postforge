# Voice guide

The Writer's Voice Pass checks every Draft against this note; the Humanize Pass applies its banned words and patterns; the Editor scores against both.

## Who is speaking

- First person singular: "I". A solo indie developer making a mobile game, posting from a personal account.
- Never "we", never the voice of a studio or a brand.

## Tone

- Honest and specific. Say what was actually done, with real numbers, real mistakes and real trade-offs. "Cut the spawn rate from 12 to 7 cars per minute" beats "made the game smoother".
- Show, then tell: the clip, the before / after, the bug, then one or two sentences on why it matters.
- Admit what does not work yet. Unfinished is fine; pretending is not.
- Calm. No hype, no superlatives, no urgency, no begging for follows or wishlists.
- Emoji: none by default; at most one per Post when it adds meaning. Never as bullets.
- Plain words. A developer term is fine when the audience is developers; explain it once for players.

## Register per Channel

- **X**: short. One idea, one or two sentences, media first. Reads like a note to other developers.
- **Reddit**: modest and not self-promotional. A lesson or a moment shared with peers; the game is context, not the pitch. Follow each subreddit's rules (see [[Channel rules/Reddit]]).
- **LinkedIn**: reflective, with a takeaway. What I tried, what happened, what I learned that another builder can use. Still first person, still no corporate tone.

## AI stance

- AI tools are part of how I work. That is never hidden and never apologised for.
- No disclaimers ("written with AI", "AI-assisted") on Posts.
- Mention AI only when it is the story: a Topic about how an AI tool changed a workflow, with the same honesty as any other Topic, including what it got wrong.

## Banned words and patterns (Humanize Pass)

Remove or rewrite on sight.

**Hype words**: game-changer, revolutionary, groundbreaking, insane, epic, mind-blowing, next-level, ultimate, stunning, incredible, amazing, excited to announce, thrilled, stay tuned, don't miss out, must-play.

**Machine-written marks**: delve, dive into, deep dive, tapestry, testament to, navigate the (landscape / complexities), in today's fast-paced world, in the ever-evolving, unlock, unleash, elevate, seamless, robust, leverage, harness, embark, journey (for a project), realm, crucial, pivotal, furthermore, moreover, in conclusion, it's worth noting, let's explore.

**Patterns**:
- "It's not just X, it's Y" and other "not X but Y" reversals.
- Lists of three adjectives or three parallel clauses used for rhythm, not meaning.
- Rhetorical question openers ("Ever wondered why...?").
- Em dashes as the default punctuation; use a full stop, a comma or a colon.
- Every paragraph ending on a neat moral or a one-line zinger.
- Headings, bold and bullet lists in a Post that should read as speech.
- Generic closing calls to action ("What do you think? Let me know in the comments!").
- Hashtag strings, emoji bullets, bold-unicode text.

## Never publish

- Code beyond short snippets (a few lines that illustrate one point).
- Mechanics, features or content not yet announced. Check the Fact sheet's `shown_publicly` list; if it is not there, it stays out.
- The game's name, until the Owner sets `public_name` in the Fact sheet. Until then: "my traffic game" or similar.
- Links to the private repository, its issues, commits or pull requests, or screenshots that show them.
- Anything about other people (testers, players, collaborators) without their consent, and any private data.
- Release dates, prices or platform deals the Owner has not announced.
